How it works

From a channel's back catalogue to published, credited clips.

1. Learn the channel first

Before anything is cut, we study the whole channel: what it is about, its running storylines, its recurring subjects, and which of its videos actually perform. A moment cut in isolation can miss a payoff that lands three videos later, so no clip is made from a video we do not understand in context.

2. Select moments, not slices

Moments are chosen across the entire catalogue rather than video by video. A clip is not necessarily one continuous chunk of one video. The strongest version of a story sometimes stitches a hook from one point to a payoff from another. Length follows the content: if a moment lands in eleven seconds, that is the clip.

3. Cut, caption, credit

  • Clips are cut vertically with burned-in captions, checked against the creator's actual words.
  • Every clip ends on a short card carrying the creator's branding.
  • Every clip credits its source and links viewers to the original video and channel.

4. Write for each platform separately

Each clip gets its own caption and hashtags per platform. TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook and X are different audiences with different conventions, and one caption pasted five times serves none of them. Captions never spoil the full video; they exist to make someone want to watch it.

5. Review before anything publishes

Every clip passes review before it is published: that the source material is covered by the creator's permission, that any licensed music or third-party footage inside a window has been flagged and dealt with, that captions claim nothing the footage does not support, and that the final render matches what was intended. Anything questionable is held for a human decision rather than posted.

6. Publish and measure

Published clips are tracked per platform, so the creator can see what their catalogue is earning in short-form. Reporting is part of the work from the start.

The publishing tool

Clips are published through our own self-hosted desktop software, registered with each platform under the name Leo Clips Cars. It runs on the operator's own computer and uploads finished clips to accounts the operator runs on TikTok, YouTube, Instagram and Facebook.

  • Every upload is started deliberately by the operator. Nothing runs unattended.
  • It posts only to accounts the operator owns and has personally connected.
  • It reads nothing from other people's accounts and collects no data about them.

Accounts are connected through each platform's own official sign-in flow. The operator signs in on the platform's website and approves access there; the tool receives an access token, stored on the operator's own computer and used only to upload clips to that account. No account passwords are stored by this tool, and none are needed.
