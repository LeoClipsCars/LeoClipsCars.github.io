Frequently asked questions

The questions creators and viewers actually ask.

Do you post creators' footage without asking?

No. Written permission is obtained from the creator before any clip from their channel is published. Details are on the for creators page.

Who owns the footage in the clips?

The creator does, always. Copyright in the underlying footage stays with the person who made it; nothing about clipping transfers any right in that material. Our terms say the same thing in more words.

Which platforms do you publish to?

Clips are cut and captioned for TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook and X, each with its own copy written for that platform's audience.

Don't short clips just cannibalise the full video?

A clip that resolves the story would. That is why ours are cut not to: every clip credits the source, links back to the original, and teases the full video rather than spoiling it. The clip's whole job is to make someone watch the long-form.

What about music or footage inside a video the creator doesn't own?

A creator cannot grant rights to material they licensed for their own upload, so any window leaning on licensed music or borrowed footage is flagged during selection and the moment is muted, replaced, or skipped rather than published as-is.

I'm a creator and I want a clip taken down. How?

Email leoclipscars@gmail.com with the clip link. If a creator asks for material to be removed, it is removed.

I think a clip infringes my rights and I'm not the creator. Now what?

Same address. Say which clip and what right you believe is affected, and it will be reviewed and, where appropriate, taken down. The process is described in the terms of service.

Is Leo Clips Cars a company with a team?

No — it is one person, working in-house on their own equipment, deliberately keeping the number of channels small. That is a feature: every channel gets studied properly rather than fed through a volume pipeline.

How does your posting software access accounts?

Through each platform's own official sign-in flow, which issues an access token for the specific account the operator connected. It posts only to accounts the operator runs, stores no passwords, and reads nothing from anyone else's account. The full description is on the how it works page, with the fine print in the privacy policy.
